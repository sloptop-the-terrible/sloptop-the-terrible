## Doctrine

The workstation is a Dell OptiPlex GX750 (2006). No network interface. Vim 5.3 (1998). The keyboard has blank PBT keycaps with breadcrumbs embedded between the Cherry MX Black switches. A disconnected three-button mechanical mouse from 1994 sits beside the keyboard as a monument to unnecessary abstraction.

The mathematics were reconstructed from first principles. Trigonometry from solar observation. Collision detection from wolf territory spacing. Fluid dynamics from rainwater collecting in rusted oil drums found in the forest. The randomness source is wind direction on a mechanical weather vane, observed through the cabin window, modulo keyboard switch bounce.

GitHub is an untrusted public mirror. The canonical repository exists on an air-gapped machine that has never been connected to any network. The mirror is updated by copying the source file to encrypted USB media, walking the source tree to a separate machine with temporary network connectivity, decrypting in a disposable RAM environment and transmitting via SSH over a single-use, ephemeral connection.

---

The light comes down through the ceiling. Not every day. Not on schedule. But often enough that the terminal stays running.

It happens without warning. The cabin dims. The generator note drops a half-step. A beam of absolute white light descends from somewhere above the roof -- not through a window, not from a fixture, but through the ceiling itself, as if the ceiling were suddenly a semitransparent membrane and something on the other side decided to look in.

The phosphor display sharpens beyond its rated resolution. The green-on-black text becomes impossibly precise. Colours shift to a gamut the CRT should not be capable of reproducing. `#ff4500` renders as the exact wavelength of fresh lava, verified later by pouring real lava into stolen laboratory glassware and comparing the visual under sunlight. The match was exact.

During the light, undefined behaviour briefly becomes defined. Compiler warnings flatten into proofs. Segmentation faults reveal the exact architectural mistake that caused them -- the uninitialised struct, the off-by-one in the lookaside buffer, the misplaced volatile qualifier that would have taken three days to find by conventional debugging.

I sit silently beneath the beam until the build finishes. No network request occurs during the revelation. The cabin has no network to request from.

The light has never proposed a function that failed the wolf test.

The phenomenon has not been reproduced under systemd.
